# Poppleton's 1822 Plan of Baltimore, georeferenced

**Source.** Thomas H. Poppleton, *Plan of the city of Baltimore*, published by
Fielding Lucas Jr., 1822. Library of Congress, call number G3844.B2 1822 .L8,
<https://www.loc.gov/item/2002624027/>, accessed 2026-08-08. Local scan
`data/raw/maps/baltimore_1822_poppleton.jp2`, 6,975 x 5,506 px.

**Why this sheet.** Poppleton was commissioned in 1818 to survey and fix the
city's streets. This is the printed result of that survey, the legal plat the
city was actually laid out from, and it is contemporary with the 1819 and 1822
directory cohorts, the two years this project places worst.

**Reproduce with** `./.venv/bin/python scripts/georeference_1822.py`.

## Control point count and spread

Thirty-three intersections were located by eye on the full-resolution scan.
Twenty-eight survive into the fit. The five that do not are listed under
"What was dropped" below.

Every pixel coordinate was read by cropping the neighbourhood out of the scan
with Pillow and looking at the crop. The crop box used for each point is stored
in the `crop` column of `gcps_1822.csv`, so any read can be repeated and
disputed. No control point was inferred from a grid model, and none was placed
on a feature that was not visible.

Coverage, in scan pixels, runs x = 1,874 to 4,790 and y = 1,001 to 3,549 on a
6,975 x 5,506 sheet.

| Direction | Extreme control point | What it anchors |
|---|---|---|
| North | Charles & Biddle, Calvert & Biddle (y = 1,001) | the northern edge of the built city |
| South | Charles & Clement, Hanover & Clement (y = 3,549) | South Baltimore below Federal Hill |
| West | Amity & Baltimore (x = 1,874) | the west end of the built-up grid |
| East | Washington & Aliceanna (x = 4,790) | eastern Fells Point |
| Centre | Charles & Baltimore, Calvert & Baltimore | the old core |

Split at the sheet centre, the network is 8 points north-west, 8 north-east,
9 south-west, 3 south-east. Nine of the 28 sit on Baltimore Street. That is
still the single most-represented street, but it is a third of the network
rather than the seven twelfths that made the 1851 attempt nearly collinear, and
the north-south axis is now carried by real spread rather than by two points.

### Where the network does not reach

Four parts of the sheet have no control near them, and the fit there is
extrapolation, not interpolation.

1. **The north-west projected grid.** Poppleton drew the quadrant north-west of
   Dolphin Street, around Pennsylvania Avenue, as a diagonal lattice of streets
   that were platted but not yet built. The district as actually built does not
   follow that lattice, so its corners are not the same corners and cannot be
   used. This is the largest hole.
2. **Canton**, east of Wolfe Street, likewise platted rather than built.
3. **Locust Point and the Federal Hill peninsula** below Clement Street.
4. **The sheet margins**, which carry no city at all.

Anyone using the warped raster in those four places should treat it as
indicative only.

## Fit RMSE against leave-one-out error

Three transforms were fit from scan pixels to EPSG:6487 metres and compared on
the same 28 points.

| Transform | Parameters per axis | Fit RMSE | LOOCV RMSE | LOOCV median | LOOCV worst |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Affine (1st order) | 3 | 21.4 m | 24.4 m | 23.0 m | 48.1 m |
| Polynomial (2nd order) | 6 | 12.6 m | 15.3 m | 12.4 m | 38.0 m |
| Thin plate spline | interpolating | 0.0 m | **13.3 m** | 9.7 m | 34.1 m |

Read the first column with care. Fit RMSE is training error: it is measured on
the same points that determined the transform, so it is optimistically biased
by construction, and the more flexible the model the more optimistic it gets.
The thin plate spline's fit RMSE of 0.0 m is not an accuracy figure at all. A
TPS passes exactly through every control point it was given, so its fit
residual is zero for any data whatsoever. Quoting it as accuracy would be
meaningless.

The leave-one-out column is the honest one. Each point is held out, the
transform is refit on the remaining 27, and the held-out point is predicted.
The gap between the two columns is the size of the optimism: 3 m for the
affine, 2.7 m for the second-order polynomial, and the whole 13.3 m for the TPS.

Per-point residuals and per-point leave-one-out errors for all three transforms
are in `gcps_1822.csv`, one row per point, so an individual bad point is
visible rather than buried in an average.

### Which transform was shipped, and why

The thin plate spline. It has the lowest leave-one-out error, and, more
importantly, it is also the best behaved outside the control network. Fitting
the three models and comparing their predictions away from the control points:

| Location on the sheet | 2nd-order polynomial vs affine | TPS vs affine |
|---|---|---|
| North-west projected grid | 58 m | 36 m |
| Canton, far east | 69 m | 27 m |
| Locust Point, south | 72 m | 46 m |
| North-west sheet corner | 279 m | 79 m |
| South-west sheet corner | 344 m | 53 m |

The second-order polynomial is the trap here. Its leave-one-out error inside
the network is respectable, but its quadratic terms run away in the corners,
by hundreds of metres. The TPS used here has a linear polynomial tail, so far
from the control points it degrades toward the affine rather than exploding.
Lowest cross-validated error and graceful extrapolation happen to point the
same way, which is the only reason this choice is comfortable.

### Where the fit is weak

The seven worst points by leave-one-out error under the shipped TPS:

| Point | LOOCV error | Why, most likely |
|---|---|---|
| Eutaw & Madison | 34.1 m | sits at the north-west edge of the network, next to the projected grid that could not be controlled |
| Calvert & Biddle | 20.8 m | northern edge, only Charles & Biddle nearby to constrain it |
| Broadway & Baltimore | 19.7 m | dense cluster of Broadway points, so holding one out leaves the others to over-smooth |
| Charles & Centre | 19.2 m | Mount Vernon Place interrupts both streets on the sheet |
| Eden & Baltimore | 18.0 m | Old Town's irregular blocks, narrow street corridors, hardest area to read |
| Caroline & Baltimore | 17.9 m | as above |
| Broadway & Aliceanna | 17.1 m | Fells Point, and the corner where HUE and modern themselves disagree most (26 m) |

The pattern is geographic and it is the expected one. Error is lowest in the
old core where the sheet is cleanest and the control is densest, and highest at
the northern edge and in Old Town east of the Jones Falls, where the blocks are
irregular and the drawn street corridors are narrow.

Reading error contributes to all of this. Street corridors on this sheet are
roughly 16 scan pixels wide, and a careful read of a corridor centre is good to
about 3 to 5 pixels, so 4 to 7 m of the 13.3 m is simply the width of my own
pencil.

## Circularity

The 1851 attempt drew its control coordinates from the HUE c.1930 street file,
then reported how far the georeferenced 1851 map sat from HUE. That is
circular: the transform had already been fit to make those two agree, so the
residual was a statement about the fit, not about the base layer.

This run avoids that by fitting against `data/raw/balt_streets.geojson`, a
modern Baltimore centreline file that is independent of HUE. HUE positions for
the same corners are computed separately and are only ever the thing being
measured, never the thing being fit to.

Two residual circularity risks remain and should be stated rather than papered
over.

1. **Modern is not 1822.** If a street physically moved between 1822 and today,
   fitting to its modern position forces the 1822 sheet onto a place it never
   was. This is why the control set sticks to the old core and to streets from
   the original Fells Point and South Baltimore plats, and why no point sits on
   the shoreline or on a watercourse. Baltimore's harbour was heavily filled and
   the Jones Falls was culverted and rerouted, so those are the two places a
   moved feature would be invisible in the residuals. Nothing in the network
   depends on either.
2. **The modern file is a partial extract.** It carries 912 named streets in the
   study bbox against HUE's 1,200. Greene Street and Johnson Street are simply
   absent from it, and several lines stop short of the corner they clearly form.
   That cost five control points but does not bias the ones that remain.

## Where this map disagrees with the c.1930 HUE geometry

This is the number the project actually needs, and the answer is smaller than
expected.

| Comparison | Median | 90th percentile | Max |
|---|---|---|---|
| HUE c.1930 vs modern centrelines, at the 28 control corners | 2.4 m | 8.8 m | 26.3 m |
| Georeferenced 1822 map vs HUE, at the same corners, held-out prediction | 9.9 m | 20.0 m | 35.6 m |

The second row uses the leave-one-out prediction of each corner, not the
in-sample one. With an interpolating spline the in-sample prediction *is* the
control point, so an in-sample comparison would have collapsed into the first
row and told us nothing.

To check whether this holds away from the control network, the two reference
layers were also compared directly at 26 further corners that were never used
as control and never read off the 1822 sheet, deliberately including the Jones
Falls corridor. Median disagreement 2.6 m. The largest were Eutaw & Dolphin at
12.1 m, Charles & Saratoga at 7.7 m, Holliday & Fayette at 7.7 m, Central
Avenue & Lombard at 6.9 m and Central Avenue & Baltimore at 6.7 m. In the Jones
Falls corridor specifically, Central Avenue and Exeter Street crossings with
Baltimore, Pratt and Lombard all agree to within 7 m.

**So the honest finding is that the 1822 plat, the c.1930 HUE survey and modern
centrelines all agree with one another to within a few tens of metres wherever
the same street corner can be identified in all three.** A visual overlay of
both centreline files on the warped raster confirms this: the modern and HUE
lines sit inside the drawn street corridors across downtown, Old Town and Fells
Point, and they sit almost on top of each other.

This does not match the 220 to 260 m displacement previously measured in the
Jones Falls corridor against the 1851 map. That earlier figure was produced by
a 12-point, nearly collinear, affine fit whose error was computed in-sample and
whose control coordinates came from the layer it was being compared against.
The most likely reading is that most of that 220 to 260 m was the 1851 fit's
own error, not a real offset in the base layer. That is a claim about the 1851
work, made from outside it, and it deserves a direct re-run of the 1851 sheet
with a proper control network before anyone acts on it.

### What this does not clear

Three caveats, and they matter more than the headline.

1. **Named streets are not alleys.** Every comparison above is between streets
   that survive in both a 1930s survey and a modern file. Much of the Black
   population in these directories lived on alleys, and an alley that was
   demolished, renamed or built over cannot be checked this way at all. The
   base layer being right about Baltimore Street says nothing about whether it
   is right about Welcome Alley.
2. **Filled land is untested.** No control point sits on the harbour edge or on
   a watercourse, by design. The shoreline south of Thames Street and the whole
   Inner Harbour edge moved by hundreds of metres between 1822 and now, and this
   fit says nothing about anyone placed there.
3. **The north-west and Canton are extrapolation.** See "Where the network does
   not reach" above.

## Notes on the scan itself

Two traps cost time and are recorded so they do not cost it again.

**The scan is of a dissected map**, cut into sections and mounted on linen. The
seams appear as continuous dark lines running through blocks, streets, the
decorative border and the blank margin alike. Measured on the blank top and
side margins they fall at x = 867, 1,735, 2,606, 3,478, 4,350, 5,232 and at
y = 1,275, 2,715, 4,155. The seam at x = 2,606 runs straight down Paca Street
and the one at x = 4,350 runs down Bond Street, which is why neither street
appears in the control set: the seam hides the corridor edges, and adjacent
sections may be very slightly misregistered against each other. The `near_seam`
column in the CSV flags any control point within 25 px of a seam.

**Harford Run** is drawn as a hatched vertical band at about x = 4,090 and
reads like a street at low zoom. It is a watercourse, later culverted under
Central Avenue, and is not a control feature.

## What was dropped, and why

Five points were read off the sheet but could not be carried into the fit
because the modern reference has no coordinate for them: Pine & Franklin and
Pearl & Franklin (both streets present, but the digitised lines stop about 45 m
short of each other), Wolfe & Bank (the modern Bank Street extract does not
reach west to Wolfe), Hanover & Conway, and Scott & Baltimore.

The script will snap two reference lines together when the gap between them is
under 30 m, on the grounds that a 5 m gap in a partial extract is a digitising
artefact rather than a real absence, and it records the gap it used in the
`snap_m` column. In this run no surviving control point needed it. Every
`snap_m` value in the shipped CSV is zero, and the gaps that did occur were all
too wide to snap, which is why those points were dropped instead.

Others were rejected before that stage:

- **Greene & Baltimore**, read cleanly at (2,508, 2,186), but Greene Street is
  absent from the modern file. Falling back to HUE for this one point would
  have reintroduced exactly the circularity this script exists to avoid.
- **Paca & Baltimore** and **Bond & Aliceanna**, both sitting on a dissection
  seam.
- **Sharp & Baltimore**. The sheet shows North Sharp and South Sharp crossing
  Baltimore Street. North of Baltimore that line is Liberty Street today, so a
  modern Sharp Street intersection is not the same corner.
- **The Washington Monument and the Battle Monument.** Both were found on the
  sheet, at (3,076, 1,501) and near (3,252, 2,108), and both still stand with
  surveyed modern coordinates in `data/raw/mihp_baltimore.geojson`. They were
  left out because the map draws them as small reference squares whose
  relationship to the modern monument point is a guess of perhaps 20 m, which
  is worse than the street corners already covering those two spots.

## Verdict

Good enough to re-place 1819 and 1822 residents against, with two conditions.

The transform is defensible: 28 visually confirmed control points spread over
the built city, a cross-validated error of 13.3 m, and a shipped model that
degrades gracefully rather than exploding at the sheet edges. Thirteen metres
is roughly half a Baltimore block, which is the right order for block-face
placement and too coarse for house-number placement.

The conditions. First, it only helps where the network reaches. Anyone placed
in the north-west quadrant, in Canton or on filled harbour land is being placed
by extrapolation and should carry a lower confidence flag. Second, and more
awkwardly, this exercise did not find the base-layer problem it was sent to
measure. Where a corner can be identified in all three sources, the 1822 plat,
HUE and modern centrelines agree to within a few tens of metres. If residents
are landing in the wrong place, on this evidence the cause is more likely to be
in the alley geometry, the house-number interpolation or the 1851 georeference
than in HUE's placement of the main street grid.
