# Georeferencing the 1836 Lucas Plan of the City of Baltimore

Status: done. Assessed 2026-08-08.

## Source

| | |
|---|---|
| Title | Plan of the city of Baltimore |
| Maker | Fielding Lucas, Jr. |
| Imprint | Baltimore : published by F. Lucas Jr., [1836] |
| Sheet | 1 map : hand colored ; 50 x 67 cm |
| Holding | Library of Congress, Geography and Map Division |
| Item page | https://www.loc.gov/item/2002624026/ |
| Master scan | https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gmd/gmd384/g3844/g3844b/wd000016.jp2 |
| Size | 6923 x 5374 px JP2, 7.35 MB |
| Accessed | 2026-08-08 |

The full-resolution scan was obtained. Everything below was measured on it, not
on the 1731 x 1344 preview already in the repo. The LOC item JSON is served
behind Cloudflare and had to be fetched through the headless browser on port
9222, but the JP2 itself downloads with a plain request. `georeference_1836.py`
downloads it automatically if it is not on disk.

This map matters because it sits in the hole between our 1822 and 1851 sheets
and is the nearest period base layer to the 1842 and 1845 directory cohorts.

## Headline

**42 control points. Leave-one-out cross validated error 21 m for the affine
fit, 18 m for a second-order polynomial, 15 m for a thin plate spline.** Fit
RMSE is 20 m, 16 m and 0 m respectively.

The shipped GeoTIFF uses the **affine** fit, so the number to quote for it is
**21 m LOO RMSE, 17 m median, 37 m worst point**.

That is much better than the 1851 pass (56 m fit RMSE on 12 clustered points),
and the comparison is not really fair to 1851: the difference is mostly that
this pass has 42 points spread over the whole sheet and read at 2x to 6x zoom
with a labelled pixel grid, where 1851 had 12 points, seven of them on one
street, read off a downsampled preview. Some of it is probably also that Lucas
worked from the city's own survey and drew a genuinely tight sheet.

A visual check confirms the number. Overlaying the HUE centrelines on the
warped raster puts them on the drawn streets across downtown, Fells Point,
South Baltimore and the west side, not a block away.

## The gap between fit RMSE and cross-validated error

| transform | fit RMSE | LOO RMSE | LOO median | LOO max |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| affine (order 1) | 19.7 m | 21.4 m | 17.4 m | 37.4 m |
| polynomial (order 2) | 15.8 m | 18.5 m | 14.4 m | 34.7 m |
| thin plate spline | 0.0 m | 15.1 m | 10.3 m | 30.4 m |

Fit RMSE grades a transform on the same points that set its coefficients, so it
is optimistic by construction. The honest number is the leave-one-out figure:
fit on 41 points, predict the 42nd, repeat 42 times.

For the affine the gap is small (19.7 to 21.4 m) because three parameters per
axis cannot chase 42 points. For the second-order polynomial it is larger
(15.8 to 18.5 m) because six parameters per axis can. **For the thin plate
spline the fit RMSE is exactly zero and means nothing at all.** A TPS
interpolates its own control points by definition, so its residual there is
zero however bad the transform is elsewhere. Only its LOO column is evidence.

## Which transform is shipped, and why not the best one

The GeoTIFF uses the affine, not the TPS that scores 6 m better.

The LOO figures only describe behaviour *inside* the control point hull, which
covers pixel x 1083 to 4901 of 6923 and y 537 to 3347 of 5374. Outside it sit
Whetstone Point, the Middle Branch, the Locust Point peninsula, the far north
above North Avenue and the sheet's decorative border. A quadratic or a spline
is free to do anything out there and generally does. An affine keeps the sheet
rectangular and degrades in a way you can predict. The 6 m is not worth it for
a base layer people will pan around.

`data/work/maps/gcps_1836.csv` carries the residual and the leave-one-out error
for all three transforms at every point, and `baltimore_1836.points` is in QGIS
Georeferencer format, so anyone who wants the TPS can rebuild it in one command
and knows exactly what they are trading.

## Control point spread

42 points, all street-street intersections, deliberately distributed rather
than piled downtown:

| region | n | what it covers |
|---|---:|---|
| NW | 6 | Fulton, Gilmor and Carey at Mosher and Lanvale, the platted grid above Franklin Square |
| N | 5 | Charles Street corridor at Biddle, Chase, Eager and Madison, plus St Paul |
| NE | 5 | Broadway, Wolfe and Chester at Lanvale, Federal, Madison, Monument |
| W | 4 | Fulton, Mount, Gilmor and Carey at Baltimore Street |
| C | 5 | Eutaw, Charles and Gay at Baltimore, Light and Gay at Pratt |
| E | 8 | Broadway, Wolfe, Washington, Chester, Ann, Bond in Old Town and Fells Point |
| S | 9 | Charles, Light, Hanover, William at York, Hughes, Montgomery, Hamburg, Cross, West |

North to south the points run from Lanvale Street (px y 537) down to West
Street in South Baltimore (px y 3347), which is 3.7 km of the map's 4.2 km of
built-up extent. West to east they run from Fulton Street (px x 1083) to
Chester Street in Canton (px x 4901), 5.0 km. Downtown holds only 5 of the 42.
That was the point. The 1851 pass put 7 of 12 on Baltimore Street and could
therefore say nothing about north-south error, and this pass was built to not
repeat that.

Nothing here was placed from a street's expected position. Every crossing was
seen on the scan: crop the neighbourhood with Pillow, upscale to about 1500 px
wide, overlay a labelled 25 or 50 px grid, read the crossing off that. Reading
precision is roughly plus or minus 5 source px, which at 1.31 m/px is about 7 m,
so about a third of the reported error is my eye on the paper rather than the
map disagreeing with the world.

Six points carry a `note` in the CSV because the street NAME was not printed at
the crossing itself. Four of those are the west Baltimore Street set: Fulton,
Mount, Gilmor and Carey are labelled where they enter the platted grid a
kilometre north, and they are drawn dead straight, so their crossings with
Baltimore Street were identified by following the same line down and matching
the visible block gap. Those four are the least certain points in the set and
they show it, averaging 25 m against 19 m for everything else. Treat the far
west with a little extra suspicion.

## Points located and then dropped rather than forced

- **Charles & Monument**, Mount Vernon Place, would have been the single best
  north-central anchor. It was found on the sheet at about px (3067, 1477) and
  dropped because HUE has a real gap in Charles Street through the Washington
  Monument plaza and the two reference lines never meet. This is the same gap
  that defeated the 1851 pass.
- **Calvert & Baltimore**. Calvert is labelled at px x 3234 near y 1600, not at
  the Baltimore Street crossing. Carrying the name 570 px south would have been
  an inference rather than a reading, and downtown is already the best covered
  part of the sheet, so it was left out.
- **Ann & Oliver**. Both names are printed, but HUE's Ann (N) and Oliver (E) do
  not intersect inside the 1846-1860 city boundary, so there is no reference
  coordinate.
- **Charles & Read** and **Charles & Centre**, same failure as Charles &
  Monument.

## Where the fit is weak

Worst leave-one-out errors under the affine:

| point | region | LOO affine | LOO poly2 | LOO TPS |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Light & West | S | 37.4 m | 17.4 m | 7.0 m |
| Ann & Aliceanna | E | 37.0 m | 32.8 m | 25.2 m |
| Mount & Baltimore | W | 35.8 m | 27.6 m | 6.5 m |
| Light & Cross | S | 34.6 m | 18.3 m | 5.1 m |
| Carey & Mosher | NW | 33.3 m | 33.2 m | 13.0 m |
| Wolfe & Lanvale | NE | 31.5 m | 13.9 m | 18.8 m |
| Bond & Aliceanna | E | 30.8 m | 27.1 m | 18.6 m |

Two different things are going on in that list.

**Points that collapse under the higher-order fits** (Light & West, Light &
Cross, Mount & Baltimore, Wolfe & Lanvale) are not bad readings. They are
places where the sheet has a smooth local warp that an affine cannot follow and
a quadratic or spline can. Lithographic stretch on a hand-pulled sheet does
exactly this. These are a cost of shipping the affine, not evidence of error in
the map or in my eye.

**Points that stay bad under every fit** are the ones to be careful with.
Carey & Mosher (33 m affine, 33 m poly2, 13 m TPS) and Ann & Aliceanna (37, 33,
25) are the two clearest. Carey & Mosher sits in the platted-but-unbuilt
north-west, where the draughtsman was drawing a projection off the city plat
rather than surveying anything, and its neighbours Fulton and Gilmor at the
same two streets score 9 m, which points at Carey specifically rather than at
the block. Ann & Aliceanna is one street back from the Fells Point wharves,
and is the single worst point in the set under both polynomial fits.

By region, mean magnitude of the affine residual:

| region | n | mean dE | mean dN | mean magnitude | worst |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| NW | 6 | -4.9 m | -4.2 m | 14.8 m | 30.4 m |
| N | 5 | +2.2 m | +8.0 m | 13.2 m | 18.4 m |
| NE | 5 | +0.4 m | -12.0 m | 16.4 m | 26.8 m |
| W | 4 | +13.3 m | +14.0 m | 24.9 m | 32.7 m |
| C | 5 | -11.8 m | -1.6 m | 15.1 m | 24.3 m |
| E | 8 | +6.8 m | +14.4 m | 21.3 m | 34.3 m |
| S | 9 | -3.5 m | -13.1 m | 16.4 m | 34.1 m |

Positive dE means the 1836 sheet puts the corner east of where the c.1930
survey puts it. The pattern is a mild rotational one, east and west both
pushed north and the middle pulled south, which is the signature of a sheet
that is very slightly bowed rather than of a mapmaker who got a neighbourhood
wrong.

**Areas with no control at all, and therefore no accuracy claim:** the Locust
Point and Whetstone Point peninsula, the Middle Branch and Ridgely's Cove, the
Canton waterfront east of Chester Street, and everything above North Avenue.
The "South Baltimore" panel at the bottom left of the sheet is an inset drawn
at its own scale and is not georeferenced by this transform at all. Do not read
anything off it.

## Where this map disagrees with the c.1930 geometry

The honest summary is that it mostly does not, and that is the finding.

After a single global affine, the 1836 street grid sits within 13 to 25 m of
the HUE c.1930 grid everywhere we have control, and about 7 m of that is my
reading of the paper. There is no region on this sheet where 1836 and 1930
disagree by the hundreds of metres that motivated this project. Across the
built-up city of 1836, the streets simply did not move.

**The Jones Falls corridor, which was the main worry, is fine.** Four points
read along the drawn centre of the Falls channel between Read Street and
Monument Street transform to within 3 to 21 m of the modern Fallsway
centreline, which is the road built on top of the Falls after it was culverted
in the 1910s. In other words the channel is where the Fallsway now is, to
inside this map's own error budget. That is the same conclusion the 1851 pass
reached (33 to 45 m to the Fallsway), reached independently and rather more
tightly.

| Falls channel point (px) | transformed (EPSG:6487) | to Fallsway | to Holliday St |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| 3378, 1375 | 433560, 181269 | 21 m | 25 m |
| 3387, 1425 | 433576, 181204 | 13 m | 0 m |
| 3406, 1475 | 433605, 181140 | 3 m | 25 m |
| 3421, 1525 | 433629, 181076 | 11 m | 46 m |

The 1851 pass reported 220 to 260 m between the drawn Falls and HUE's Front
Street and read it as displacement. This pass suggests a plainer explanation.
HUE's Front Street survives only as two short fragments, the northern one
spanning y 180622 to 180906, and the Falls points measured here are at y
181076 to 181269, north of where that fragment ends. The distances that come
out of a nearest-point query are therefore mostly distance along a street that
is not digitised there, not a perpendicular offset. **The Falls corridor drift
is a gap in the reference data, not a 200 m movement of the ground.** The
practical consequence for the project is the opposite of what was feared:
residents placed on Falls-corridor streets are not systematically 200 m out
because the corridor moved, they are at risk because the specific alleys and
short streets they lived on were erased and have no modern line at all.

**The waterfront is the one place worth flagging.** The two Fells Point
Aliceanna Street points are the worst pair in the set under every transform
(25 to 37 m), and region E as a whole carries a mean northward residual of
14 m. That is the direction you would expect if the shoreline blocks were
rebuilt and squared up during a century of wharf work. It is tens of metres,
not hundreds, but it is the one systematic signal here that plausibly reflects
the ground changing rather than the paper.

## Circularity

The control coordinates come from the same HUE c.1930 layer that this map is
being compared against. That was deliberate and it bounds the claims.

What this can say: after removing a single global scale, rotation and shift,
where and by how much does the 1836 draughtsman's geometry disagree locally
with the 1930 survey. That is the question the project actually needs answered
and the residual table above answers it.

What this cannot say: anything about the absolute accuracy of either source. A
uniform citywide error in HUE would be absorbed entirely into the fitted affine
and would leave no trace in any residual. No georeference of this map against
HUE could ever detect it. Testing that would need control coordinates from a
source that is not HUE, such as a survey-grade modern layer or GPS on surviving
1836 structures, and is a separate job.

One second-order point. `geocode_1860.load_streets()` layers modern centrelines
underneath HUE to supply names HUE lacks, so a handful of these control points
may be resolved against modern rather than c.1930 geometry. For the stable
downtown core the two sources agree closely, so this does not change any
conclusion, but it does mean "c.1930" is a slight simplification of what the
reference layer is.

## Honest verdict

Good enough to re-place residents against, with two conditions.

Twenty-one metres is roughly half a Baltimore block. That is enough to say
which block and which ward a person was in, enough to compare directory
placement against period geometry, and enough to overlay on the site as a
period base layer for the 1842 and 1845 cohorts. It is not enough to claim
which side of a street a specific address was on, and it never will be from a
lithograph.

The two conditions. First, use it only inside the control hull. The peninsulas,
the Middle Branch and everything above North Avenue have no control and no
accuracy claim. Second, and more importantly, this map's accuracy is not the
binding constraint on placing residents. Twenty-one metres of base-layer error
sits underneath a much larger geocoding error from interpolating house numbers
along a street between anchors. Swapping the base layer for this one removes a
source of error that, on the evidence here, was never the large one. The real
gain from this exercise is the negative result: for the built-up city of 1836,
the c.1930 street file is not badly wrong, so the project's existing placements
do not need to be redone on geometric grounds.

## Reproducing

```
./.venv/bin/python scripts/georeference_1836.py
```

Downloads the LOC master JP2 to `data/raw/maps/baltimore_1836_plan.jp2` if it
is absent (set `BALT_1836_JP2` to point somewhere else), resolves the control
points against `geocode_1860.load_streets()`, fits and cross validates all
three transforms, prints the residual and displacement tables above, and writes:

- `data/work/maps/gcps_1836.csv`, 42 points with per-point residuals and
  per-point leave-one-out errors for all three transforms
- `data/work/maps/baltimore_1836.points`, QGIS Georeferencer format, EPSG:6487
- `data/work/maps/baltimore_1836_georef.tif`, EPSG:6487, 7207 x 5641 px,
  1.32 m/px, 9.2 MB, JPEG compressed and tiled, affine fit

Fitted affine: 1.3097 m/px, rotation 2.725 degrees, sheet 9.1 x 7.0 km. The
rotation is close to the 2.67 degrees the 1851 sheet gave, which is a small
independent sanity check on both. The scale agrees with the map's own printed
scale bar, which reads about 1.36 m/px measured on the scan.
