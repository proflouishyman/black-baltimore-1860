# Georeferencing the 1851 Sidney & Neff Plan of the City of Baltimore

Status: done, and it supersedes the first pass. Assessed 2026-08-08.

Script: `scripts/georeference_1851_v2.py`. The original
`scripts/georeference_1851.py` is left in place unchanged so the two can be
compared.

## Source

| | |
|---|---|
| Title | Plan of the city of Baltimore, Maryland |
| Maker | Compiled from records and surveys by Sidney & Neff |
| Imprint | Published by Lloyd Van Derveer, June 1851 |
| Holding | Library of Congress, Geography and Map Division |
| Item page | https://www.loc.gov/item/2004629026/ |
| Local copy | `data/raw/maps/baltimore_1851_plan.jp2` |
| Size | 13,414 x 10,643 px JP2, 20.1 MB |
| Accessed | scan already on disk; item page checked 2026-08-08 |

The full-resolution JP2 was used throughout. Every control point was read off it
at 2x to 6x zoom with a labelled pixel grid drawn over the crop. Nothing was
read off the 3,354 x 2,661 preview, which is what the first pass used.

At the fitted scale the sheet resolves 0.625 m per pixel, so one mile is about
2,574 px. Reading a street corridor centre to plus or minus 10 px, which is
roughly what the engraving allows, is therefore a 6 m reading uncertainty. That
is the floor under everything below.

## Headline

**54 control points. Leave-one-out cross validated error 15.9 m for the affine
fit, 13.8 m for a second-order polynomial, 11.4 m for a thin plate spline.**

The shipped GeoTIFF uses the **affine** fit, so the number to quote for it is
**15.9 m LOO RMSE, 11.3 m median, 40.9 m worst point**.

For comparison, the first pass reported 56.3 m and that figure was fit RMSE on
12 points, not a cross validated error, so the true gap is wider than the
numbers suggest.

## The three faults of the first pass, and what changed

**Collinearity.** The first pass had 12 points, seven of them on Baltimore
Street. A nearly collinear network pins the east-west axis and leaves the
north-south axis almost free. This pass has 54 points reaching from Gilmor
Street in the west to Canton in the east and from Greenmount at Lanvale in the
north to Cross Street in the south. 17 of the 54 sit on Baltimore Street, and
53 distinct named streets are involved altogether.

**Training error reported as accuracy.** The first pass computed RMSE on the
same points that set the coefficients. This pass reports leave-one-out cross
validation as the headline and prints fit RMSE beside it so the gap is visible.

**Circularity.** This is the important one. The first pass took its control
coordinates from the HUE circa-1930 street file, then reported how far the
result sat from HUE. Fitting to a layer and then measuring disagreement with
that same layer is not a measurement. The fit absorbs HUE's own error into the
transform and reports whatever is left over as if it belonged to the 1851 map.

This pass takes control coordinates from the modern authoritative Baltimore City
street centreline file (911 / AddressDB StreetCity, ArcGIS REST, accessed
2026-08-08), which is independent of both the 1851 sheet and the HUE
digitisation. That makes the 1851-versus-HUE comparison at the end a real
three-way check.

The assumption it buys, and it is a genuine assumption that should be stated
rather than buried, is that a named street crossing in the built-up city has not
physically moved between 1851 and today. That holds well for the old grid and
badly for made ground, so no control point sits on a shoreline, a wharf, a
watercourse, or on Locust Point.

## Fit RMSE against cross validated error

| transform | fit RMSE | fit max | LOO RMSE | LOO median | LOO p90 | LOO max |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| affine (order 1) | 15.0 m | 39.0 m | 15.9 m | 11.3 m | 25.1 m | 40.9 m |
| polynomial (order 2) | 12.2 m | 33.1 m | 13.8 m | 9.1 m | 19.1 m | 37.6 m |
| thin plate spline | 0.0 m | 0.0 m | 11.4 m | 7.6 m | 17.8 m | 32.5 m |

The TPS fit residual is 0.0 m by construction. A thin plate spline interpolates
exactly at its control points, so its fit residual is a statement about the
algebra and not about the map. Only the leave-one-out column means anything for
TPS, and even that is optimistic outside the control hull because the kernel
grows without bound away from the points.

The affine gap is small, 15.0 m to 15.9 m, because three parameters per axis
against 54 points cannot chase noise. The order-2 gap is larger, 12.2 m to
13.8 m, and the TPS gap is total. That pattern is what tells you the extra
flexibility is buying about 4 m of real signal and spending the rest on noise.

**The affine is shipped anyway.** The 4.5 m that TPS buys is small against the
other error in this project, and an affine warp keeps the raster geometrically
honest and behaves predictably in the northwest quadrant and on Locust Point,
which are outside the control hull. The GCP table carries the TPS columns for
anyone who wants to rewarp.

## Control point spread

54 points. Pixel extent 2,989 to 12,528 in x, 988 to 6,653 in y, on a
13,414 x 10,643 sheet.

| region | n | mean east offset | mean north offset | mean magnitude |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| core (downtown) | 10 | -6.0 m | +4.6 m | 13.6 m |
| west (Poppleton to Gilmor) | 7 | -0.5 m | -16.0 m | 17.1 m |
| east (Caroline to Luzerne) | 8 | -1.2 m | -6.7 m | 7.8 m |
| north (St Paul to Greenmount) | 7 | +5.0 m | +7.5 m | 10.8 m |
| northeast (Monument St east, Harford) | 5 | -1.4 m | -5.0 m | 10.3 m |
| northwest (Etting St) | 2 | +1.7 m | -0.3 m | 4.0 m |
| Fells Point | 3 | -2.5 m | +4.6 m | 6.1 m |
| south (Federal Hill) | 7 | +7.8 m | +15.0 m | 25.7 m |
| Canton | 5 | -1.0 m | -5.0 m | 5.4 m |

Coverage in words. East-west coverage is good and reaches both edges of the
built-up area. North-south coverage is adequate but thinner than east-west: the
northernmost point is Greenmount at Lanvale (y = 988) and the southernmost is
the Cross Street group (y = 6,653), with Canton at y = 6,507 anchoring the
southeast. The convex hull of the control net covers about 35.5 of the sheet's
143 million pixels, which sounds thin until you notice that most of the rest of
the sheet is water, cemetery, or open country.

**The real gap is the northwest quadrant.** No point was taken west of Etting
Street north of Franklin Street, which leaves wards 19 and 20 (Bolton Hill,
Madison Park, the Pennsylvania Avenue corridor) unconstrained. The reason is in
the next section. Anything placed up there is extrapolating, and the affine will
be carrying whatever local distortion that corner of the sheet has without
anything to correct it.

## Where the fit is weak

**South Baltimore is the worst region**, mean magnitude 25.7 m, and it is
systematically biased north and east. The two largest residuals in the whole set
are there: Light & Hughes at 40.9 m LOO and Charles & Cross at 38.6 m. Two
things are going on. Light Street on the sheet runs almost straight north-south
between Hughes and Cross, where the modern survey has it drifting 51 m east over
that span. And Charles Street runs directly under the sheet join, which puts a
crease of mottled paper about 30 px wide right where the street should be, so
its x coordinate there is the weakest reading on the sheet. Both points were
kept rather than trimmed, because dropping the worst points is how a fit gets
flattered.

**The west side is biased 16 m north**, uniformly across Gilmor, Stricker,
Carey and Poppleton. That is a real local stretch in the sheet, not a single bad
reading, and an affine cannot absorb it.

**The sheet join.** This map is printed on two sheets joined vertically at
about x = 6,680, and the crease runs the full height. It falls on or immediately
beside Charles Street for most of that run. Three control points sit on it
(Charles & Baltimore, Charles & Franklin, Charles & Cross) and are flagged in
the GCP table. The join itself is well registered, the ornamental border matches
across it, so this is a legibility problem rather than a misalignment problem.

**Not used, and why.** Recording these matters as much as recording what was
used.

| excluded | reason |
|---|---|
| Fort Ave & Lawrence St and the rest of Locust Point | still largely a paper subdivision in 1851, and the whole peninsula was later filled and re-platted for the railway terminal. The 1851 lot lines there cannot be assumed to be today's streets. |
| Thames St & Bond St and the Fells Point wharf line | filled and rebuilt. Waterfront points measure made ground, not surveyed streets. |
| Pennsylvania Ave and the Bolton Hill / Madison Park grid | speculative lot layout over ground that was mostly unbuilt in 1851, with most street names later changed. Street identities could not be confirmed one by one without assuming the answer. |
| anything on the Jones Falls or Harford Run | both culverted and rerouted. |

Street renamings were resolved against the project's own alias table,
`data/work/street_aliases.csv`, from the Gunby index. Where a whole run of
adjacent streets was renamed, as in Canton and east Baltimore, the run was
checked as a run: the 1851 Canton sequence Patapsco, Chesapeake, Harrison,
Patuxent, Curley, Potomac maps one for one onto modern Lakewood, Kenwood,
Streeper, Linwood, Curley, Potomac, with every pair landing within 25 px, and
two of the six never changed name at all. The same holds in east Baltimore,
where the 1851 run Castle, Chester, Duncan, Choptank, Madeira, Gist, Bradford,
Burke, Port, Cannon, Rose, Luzerne matches the modern spacing street for street
and Rose and Luzerne kept their names, pinning the renamed neighbours around
them.

## Where this map disagrees with the c.1930 HUE geometry

Short answer: by about 11 m, and almost all of that is the 1851 map's error
rather than HUE's.

| comparison | n | median | mean | p90 | max |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1851 georeference vs HUE, at the control crossings | 50 | 11.1 m | 12.7 m | 23.4 m | 29.6 m |
| HUE vs the modern survey, at the same crossings | 50 | 2.3 m | 3.1 m | 4.9 m | 14.7 m |
| HUE vs the modern survey, at 40 further crossings across the city | 40 | 2.6 m | 3.5 m | 6.4 m | 18.5 m |

The second and third rows are the finding. **The HUE circa-1930 street file is
not the large unquantified error source this project assumed it was.** At 40
intersections spread from Fulton Avenue to Canton and from North Avenue to
Locust Point, ten of them in the Jones Falls and Old Town corridor, HUE sits a
median of 2.6 m from the modern city survey. The worst was Pratt & Central at
18.5 m. That is an order of magnitude better than the 1851 map can be
georeferenced to.

### The 220 to 260 m Jones Falls displacement does not survive

`docs/GEOREFERENCE.md` records the 1851 map's Jones Falls channel landing 220 to
260 m from HUE's Front Street, and reads that as Front Street having drifted.
Re-measured against the new fit, it does not hold up. Five points read off the
drawn channel between Fayette Street and Fish Street transform to:

| 1851 channel pixel | to HUE Front St | to modern Front St | to modern Fallsway |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| (7878, 4100) | 73.2 m | 74.5 m | 7.0 m |
| (7970, 4250) | 46.8 m | 47.2 m | 28.4 m |
| (8000, 4350) | 47.9 m | 48.7 m | 88.1 m |
| (8023, 4500) | 49.3 m | 49.3 m | 179.7 m |
| (8035, 4625) | 73.3 m | 73.2 m | 256.6 m |

The channel sits 47 to 73 m from Front Street, which is simply the width of the
walled channel plus the block between it and the street, and HUE's Front Street
and the modern Front Street agree with each other to about 1 m all the way down.
The 1851 map's own drawn Front Street, read at two points, lands 27.0 m and
0.8 m from HUE's.

The last column shows where the old number probably came from. Distance from the
1851 channel to the modern Fallsway grows from 7 m at the north end to 257 m at
the south end, because the Fallsway does not follow the old channel below
Baltimore Street. Comparing the 1851 channel against a 20th-century expressway
alignment produces a couple of hundred metres of apparent drift that is an
artefact of the comparison, and the earlier 12-point fit, whose north-south axis
was barely constrained, would have added to it.

### What this does not say

It does not say HUE is a good 1851 or 1860 base layer. It says HUE's **geometric
registration** is good. Whether HUE contains the right streets for a given
decade, under the right names, with the alleys people actually lived on, is a
different question and this measurement cannot touch it. Note in particular that
the check can only be run on streets that still exist in the modern file, which
means the alleys HUE uniquely carries, exactly the ones that matter most for
this project, are unvalidated by it.

It also does not say residents are placed to 3 m. Placement error in this
project is dominated by interpolating a house number along a block, which is
tens of metres on its own, and by the 1880s renumbering. Base layer registration
was never the binding constraint. That is the useful thing to know.

## Verdict

**Is this good enough to re-place residents against? No, and it should not be
used for that.** At 15.9 m cross validated the 1851 sheet is a good survey, but
HUE is already registered to about 2.6 m, so swapping the base layer would make
the geometry worse, not better. The right conclusion is that the base layer was
never the problem.

**Is it good enough for the things it is actually good for? Yes.** At 15.9 m,
comfortably inside a city block, this georeference will support:

- **A period street and alley inventory.** The sheet shows which streets and
  alleys existed in 1851 and under which names. That is what HUE cannot tell
  you, and it is the input that would actually improve placement.
- **The 1851 shoreline and the harbour head**, as a mask for where nobody could
  have lived and as a check on which addresses fall on later fill.
- **Ward validation.** The sheet prints ward numbers directly, and wards 1, 2,
  7, 8, 17, 18, 19 and 20 are legible on it. At this accuracy the HUE
  `baltimore_wards_1846_1860` polygons can now be tested against a period source
  rather than assumed. That test has not been run here.
- **Exhibit basemap.** As a visual layer under the resident points it is
  accurate to well within a block everywhere inside the control hull.

**Where not to trust it.** The northwest quadrant above Franklin Street and west
of Etting Street has no control at all. Locust Point and Fort McHenry are
outside the hull and on ground that was later filled. South Baltimore carries a
systematic 15 m northward bias. Anything read within about 30 px of x = 6,680 is
sitting on the sheet join.

## Reproducing

```
./.venv/bin/python scripts/georeference_1851_v2.py
```

Downloads the modern centrelines on first run and caches them in the system temp
directory, resolves every control pair, fits all three transforms, runs
leave-one-out, writes the GCP table, and warps the GeoTIFF through the QGIS
GDAL bundle. Add `--no-tif` to skip the warp, `--points` to also emit a QGIS
Georeferencer file.
