# 1857: Robert Taylor, *Map of the City and County of Baltimore, Maryland*

Georeferenced 2026-08-08 by `scripts/georeference_1857.py`.

## Source

| | |
|---|---|

| Full-resolution scan | https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gmd/gmd384/g3843/g3843b/la000284.jp2 |
| Accessed | 2026-08-08 |
| Scan size | 15,804 × 19,291 px, JP2, 43.2 MB |
| Surveyor / publisher | Robert Taylor, "from actual surveys", lithographed by Hunckel & Son, Baltimore |
| Copyright statement on sheet | 1857 |

The sheet is a **county** land-ownership map. Baltimore City appears only as an
inset in the upper right, occupying roughly x 10,000–14,420, y 4,600–8,500 of
the full-resolution scan at about **1.65 m per pixel**. That inset is what was
georeferenced. The county map surrounding it is drawn at roughly 3.6 m per
pixel (its own scale bar reads about 442 px to the mile), so no single
transform can serve both and warping the whole sheet would be meaningless.

A note on reading the scan: the map carries a physical horizontal fold at about
y = 6,490, running straight through Fells Point and Federal Hill. It is visible
in the crops and is a candidate explanation for some of the local disagreement
in that band.

## What this map was fitted to, and why it is not HUE

Control coordinates come from **modern OpenStreetMap street centrelines**,
fetched from Overpass and cached, not from the c.1930 HUE file.

This matters. The 1851 attempt in `scripts/georeference_1851.py` resolved its
control points against HUE and then reported the result as a measurement of how
far 1851 sits from HUE. That is circular. The fit absorbs precisely the
disagreement it was meant to expose, and the residual then reports only the
map's internal noise. Fitting to an independent modern layer avoids that, and
it makes two separate, non-circular measurements possible at the same time:

1. how well the 1857 sheet can be brought onto true ground geometry, reported
   below as leave-one-out cross-validated error, and
2. how far HUE's own intersections sit from the modern ones at those same named
   crossings, reported in the `hue_vs_osm_m` column of the GCP table, computed
   without any reference to the 1857 map at all.

`data/raw/balt_streets.geojson` was tried first as the modern reference and
rejected. It is a partial, generalised extract of 4,878 features in which
several needed streets are reduced to disconnected fragments or zero-length
geometries, so most control pairs simply do not cross in it.

## Control points: count and spread

**39 control points.** Every pixel coordinate was read off the full-resolution
scan by cropping the neighbourhood with GDAL, overlaying a labelled pixel grid,
and locating the street corridors visually. None was predicted from the fit or
interpolated from a neighbour.

Coverage in scan pixels, against the inset extent:

| Direction | Control extent | Inset city content | Unconstrained margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| North | y ≥ 5,164 (Calvert & Biddle) | y ≈ 4,650 | ~510 px, about 880 m |
| South | y ≤ 7,241 (Charles & Randall) | y ≈ 8,100 | ~860 px, about 1.5 km |
| West | x ≥ 10,291 (Monroe St) | x ≈ 10,050 | ~240 px, about 400 m |
| East | x ≤ 13,296 (Wolfe St) | x ≈ 14,320 | ~1,020 px, about 1.7 km |

By quadrant the 39 points fall out as: 6 downtown core, 6 north-central
(Mount Vernon and Seton Hill), 6 east and north-east (Broadway, Wolfe,
Caroline), 4 Fells Point, 10 west (Monroe, Fulton, Poppleton, Calhoun, Carey,
Stricker), 7 south (Federal Hill and South Baltimore). No single street carries
more than 7 of the 39, and the 7 on Baltimore Street are spread across 3 km of
its length rather than clustered downtown. This is the specific failure of the
1851 attempt that had to be avoided: 7 of its 12 points sat on Baltimore Street
in a nearly collinear line, so the north-south axis was barely constrained.

The convex hull of the control points covers the built-up 1857 city. It does
**not** cover Canton, Locust Point, or the northern fringe above Biddle Street.
Those three areas are extrapolation and should not be trusted, which is a
smaller loss than it sounds because all three are drawn on this sheet as
uniform empty rectangles, that is, platted but unbuilt land.

## Accuracy

Fit RMSE is computed on the same points that set the coefficients, so it is
optimistic by construction. The thin plate spline interpolates its control
points exactly, so its fit residual is zero and carries no information at all.
Leave-one-out cross validation, refitting on the other 38 points and predicting
the held-out one, is the honest number.

| Transform | Fit RMSE | **LOO RMSE** | LOO median | LOO 90th pct | LOO max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Affine (6 param) | 39.0 m | **42.9 m** | 28.2 m | 68.2 m | 100.5 m |
| 2nd-order polynomial (12 param) | 28.9 m | **35.0 m** | 26.3 m | 54.6 m | 89.2 m |
| Thin plate spline | 0.0 m | **25.1 m** | 19.4 m | 34.7 m | 71.1 m |

**Headline: 25 m leave-one-out RMSE, 19 m median, using a thin plate spline.**

The gap between fit and LOO is small for the affine (39.0 to 42.9 m) because
six parameters cannot chase 39 points, so there is very little to overfit. It
is wider for the polynomial (28.9 to 35.0 m) and total for the spline. Anyone
quoting the spline's 0.0 m fit residual as an accuracy figure would be quoting
an identity, not a measurement.

The thin plate spline is what ships in the GeoTIFF. It wins by a real margin
and not by overfitting noise, because the error it removes is spatially
coherent rather than random: see the western stretch described below.

### Where the fit is weak

| Subset | n | Affine LOO RMSE | TPS LOO RMSE |
|---|---|---|---|
| West of Poppleton (px < 11,500) | 10 | 72.4 m | 35.4 m |
| Core and east (px ≥ 11,500) | 29 | 25.7 m | 20.4 m |

The western third of the map is roughly three times worse than the rest under
a global affine. The residual vectors show why, and they are not noise:

| Point | dEast | dNorth |
|---|---|---|
| Fulton & Franklin | −89.5 m | +1.0 m |
| Monroe & Baltimore | −74.2 m | −13.6 m |
| Monroe & Lombard | −73.4 m | −1.1 m |
| Monroe & Pratt | −72.4 m | +15.4 m |
| Poppleton & Lombard | +51.5 m | +22.1 m |
| Poppleton & Pratt | +53.0 m | +11.2 m |
| Poppleton & Baltimore | +51.7 m | −10.7 m |
| Carey & Mulberry | +58.3 m | +8.1 m |
| Stricker & Mosher | +45.0 m | −24.6 m |
| Calhoun & Lanvale | +44.6 m | −37.7 m |

Monroe and Fulton are pushed about 75 m too far west, and everything from
Poppleton out to Carey is pushed about 50 m too far east. In other words the
map compresses the ground between Monroe and Poppleton by roughly 125 m, and
does so consistently at Baltimore, Lombard and Pratt alike. This is a real
distortion in the engraved plate, not scatter, which is exactly why the spline
recovers most of it: the three Monroe points fall from 83–85 m LOO error under
the affine to 8–14 m under the spline.

Only one point stays bad under every transform. **Fulton & Franklin** is 89.6 m
off the affine fit and still 71.1 m off under the spline. It sits at the
north-western corner of the control hull with no near neighbour to constrain
the surface, and the surrounding block spacing on the map is visibly wrong:
Fulton to Mount reads about 265 m on the sheet against 122 m on the ground,
while Mount to Gilmor reads correctly. It is kept in the table rather than
quietly deleted, because deleting the worst point to improve a headline number
is how these figures get inflated.

### Points that were excluded, and why

Two intersections were read and then rejected because they are internally
inconsistent with everything east of them: **Pulaski & Baltimore** (x = 10,215)
and **Smallwood & Baltimore** (x = 10,144). Modern spacing runs Smallwood to
Pulaski 121 m, then Pulaski to Monroe 270 m. The map draws 71 px, about 117 m,
then 75 px, about 123 m. The sheet is missing roughly a block's worth of ground
between Pulaski and Monroe. Rather than let that drag the western fit, both
were dropped and the gap is reported here instead.

Three structural zones were excluded wholesale:

- **Bolton and Mount Royal**, roughly x 11,150–11,750, y 5,150–5,600. Lanvale,
  Dolphin, Preston and Biddle are all drawn running at about 40 degrees off
  east-west. Those streets run east-west in the built city. This quarter of the
  sheet is a projected plat that was never executed and cannot be matched to
  real geometry at all.
- **The far north-east** (x > 12,600, y < 5,250) and **the far south below Fort
  Avenue** (y > 7,400). Drawn as uniform empty rectangles with no building
  hatching, that is, platted but unbuilt in 1857.
- **Every waterfront point.** Thames Street, City Dock, the Fells Point piers
  and the Middle Branch shore were all filled after 1857 and the Jones Falls
  was culverted. A point there measures land reclamation, not map error.

Gay & Baltimore was also dropped. Gay Street runs diagonally through the block
there and its centreline cannot be pinned better than about 25 px, or 40 m, on
the scan.

## Where this map disagrees with the c.1930 HUE geometry

Two separate answers, because the question splits in two.

**First, HUE itself is not the problem, at least not on the main street grid.**
At the 39 control crossings, the HUE intersection sits a median of **2.3 m**
from the modern one, mean 3.3 m, maximum 11.8 m. Widening that test to every
named street present in both layers gives 2,832 intersections across the old
city with a median offset of **2.5 m**, a 90th percentile of 10.8 m and a mean
of 5.6 m. HUE's coordinates for the main grid are, to a first approximation,
correct.

The tail is where the trouble is: the 99th percentile of that broad comparison
is **58 m** and the maximum is **193 m**. Roughly one intersection in a hundred
is displaced far enough to put a resident on the wrong block. HUE is described
by its compilers as roughly half hand-digitised from period maps, and the
crossings tested here are overwhelmingly major named streets, which are the
part most likely to have come from the surveyed half. Nothing here says
anything about HUE's alleys, and the alleys are where much of this project's
Black population actually lived.

**Second, the 1857 map disagrees with HUE by essentially the same amounts it
disagrees with modern geometry**, since the two reference layers differ by only
2 to 3 m in the median. Concretely, and reading the affine residuals as
1857-versus-1930 displacement:

| Zone | Typical disagreement |
|---|---|
| Downtown core, Mount Vernon, Fells Point | 10–30 m |
| East Baltimore (Broadway to Wolfe) | 10–35 m |
| Federal Hill and South Baltimore | 10–40 m |
| West Baltimore (Monroe to Carey) | 45–90 m, spatially coherent |
| Bolton and Mount Royal | not measurable, the plat was never built |

The 220–260 m displacement measured in the Jones Falls corridor against the
1851 map is **not** reproduced anywhere on this sheet. No control point was
placed in that corridor here, deliberately, because the Falls was culverted and
rerouted and its banks are the least stable feature on any Baltimore map. But
the broad HUE-versus-modern test above does cover streets on both sides of the
corridor, and it does not show 200 m errors there. Whatever produced the 1851
figure, it is more likely to be a property of the 1851 sheet, or of the
particular features chosen to measure it, than of HUE's coordinates.

## Honest verdict

**Good enough to re-place residents against inside the built-up city, with a
stated uncertainty of about 25 m, and not good enough anywhere else on the
sheet.**

Twenty-five metres is roughly a third of a downtown Baltimore block. For an
exhibit that maps a household to a block face, that is defensible. For any
claim that turns on which side of a street or which end of a block someone
lived, it is not.

Three qualifications belong on the label:

1. The figure is 25 m only inside the control hull. West of Poppleton, expect
   35 m and be aware that the map itself compresses the ground there by more
   than 100 m in ways the spline is smoothing over rather than correcting.
   North of Biddle Street, east of Wolfe Street, and south of Randall Street
   the transform is extrapolating and no error figure applies.
2. This is accuracy against *modern* geometry, which is the right benchmark for
   truth but not the same as accuracy against 1857 reality. The map's own
   surveyor made errors that no georeferencing can undo, and the western
   stretch is probably exactly that.
3. The result is a measurement of the *map*, not a licence to re-place anyone.
   Given that HUE's main-grid coordinates turn out to agree with modern
   geometry to about 2.5 m, swapping the base layer to this 1857 sheet would
   make most placements **worse**, not better, because it would substitute a
   25 m map error for a 2.5 m survey error. The value of this sheet to the
   project is as evidence about which streets and alleys existed in 1857 and
   what they were called, not as a coordinate source.

That third point is the important one and it inverts the premise the task
started from. The largest unquantified error in this project is very probably
not the c.1930 base layer's coordinates. It is house-number interpolation along
streets, and the alleys that HUE carries but no modern layer does, neither of
which this map can fix.

Affine coefficients, for anyone who wants a stable closed-form transform rather
than the spline. Inputs are full-resolution scan pixels, outputs are metres in
EPSG:6487:

```
E = 412893.098 + 1.639027*px + 0.102949*py
N = 189476.054 + 0.095695*px - 1.716546*py
```

Ground sample distance about 1.639 m/px east-west and 1.717 m/px north-south.
The 4.5 per cent anisotropy between the two is absorbed by the affine and is
consistent with a lithographic plate that has stretched slightly more in one
direction than the other, which is ordinary for a folded sheet of this age.
