Working prototype, not a finished map. Street geometry is a c.1930 survey, the closest layer that still carries the alleys — Camel, Pin, Welcome, Strawberry — where much of this population actually lived. Modern street data has lost them, and would have quietly emptied the densest blocks. A properly georeferenced 1860s map is the next step, and will move these dots.

Drag to pan, scroll to zoom, hover a dot for the person.

Sources

Wood's Baltimore City Directory for 1860, John W. Woods. Residents and the house-number tables are taken from the scanned volume at the Internet Archive.

Street centrelines and ward boundaries: Historical Urban Ecological (HUE) Data, Center for Population Economics, ICPSR study 35617. Incorporates U.S. Census Bureau TIGER/Line data, acknowledged as the source per its terms of use.

Parsing, geocoding and cartography by Louis Hyman, Johns Hopkins University.
